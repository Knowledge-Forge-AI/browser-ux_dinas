The maintained system1-catalog.json includes the three requested products for
Chrome, Firefox, Vivaldi and Floorp, with synthetic coexistence fixtures. This is
future work and is not a prerequisite for trying the prepared personal-host trial.
The attended route can qualify individual features independently; unknown or
unsupported features retain local gaps. No broker or external security service
enters normal browsing.

The future off-host Tart lane should use disposable profiles, synthetic password,
TOTP and alias data, and local TLS fixtures. Verify official identity/update routes,
required/default-on/opt-out semantics, KeePassXC primary posture, optional blocked
Apple helpers, prompt coexistence, signing enforcement and one disconnected VPN
instance. Test localhost/native bridge access, privacy/HTTPS/DoH/content blockers,
scroll surfaces and fullscreen hover separately. Declared extensions and helper
files alone do not prove enabled, authenticated or usable behavior. No live vault,
account, cookie, pairing-code or recovery-secret content belongs in tests or reports.
SimpleLogin is optional; no real alias creation or forwarding change is authorized.

Keep synthetic Vivaldi Mail scale/regression testing separate from the already
working live Mail, Calendar and Feeds selection and operator-resolved account.
Retain the historical OOM evidence without inventing a vendor fix, universal count
threshold or unlimited scalability. No credentialed VM image, profile copy, live
mail corpus or real Proton Bridge account is used. A later safe-account Bridge
experiment needs its own explicit scope. Do not repeat the closed live Mail campaign.

Proton Authenticator is a separate native product, not a fourth installed extension.
Pass TOTP is plan-dependent and Apple Passwords supports verification codes. Never
move the Proton account's only second factor into the vault that needs it. The
catalog distinguishes optional SimpleLogin, official-catalog findings for other
Proton/iCloud services, and Windows-only iCloud Bookmarks; none expands this rollout.
